What is this?

A sandbox for your financial history. Export CSVs from schwab.com and drop them here: brokerage transaction history, checking transaction history, and positions. Everything runs in this page — data lives in this browser's localStorage, nothing is uploaded anywhere, and the page makes no network requests at all. The smiley switches to a synthetic demo dataset kept in a separate store, safe for screenshots.

Getting the data

On schwab.com: Accounts → History, pick an account and date range, then Export (top right of the table). For holdings: Accounts → Positions → Export. Exports cap at 10,000 rows, so pull long histories in chunks — overlapping chunks are fine, duplicates are detected. Optionally drop per-symbol daily price CSVs (Date,…,Close — Yahoo or Nasdaq style, named after the symbol, e.g. VTI.csv) to firm up historical valuations. The save button exports everything as one JSON backup; drop it back in to restore.

Categories

Every row gets a category: income, spending, trades, dividends, or internal transfers (matching transfer pairs across your accounts show ⇌; an unmatched ? means money left your accounts). Click a category to fix it — with always, for similar descriptions checked, one click writes a rule for the whole history: tag one paycheck as income.payroll and they all follow.

How the model works

Bank balances integrate your transactions and are anchored to the export's own RunningBalance column (drift is reported, never hidden). Brokerage cash anchors to the positions snapshot. Holdings come from trade quantities; prices come from your own trades — every buy and sell is a dated price observation — plus the positions snapshot and any price CSVs. Days valued between observations draw dashed: approximate, never passed off as exact.

Decomposition: over any window, the change in net worth splits into external flows (income minus spending) plus a market-growth residual — dividends, interest, and price moves land in the residual.

Counterfactual: the same transaction log is replayed under a policy — payroll removed after the cutoff, spending scaled by the slider, transfers and purchases scaled down when the money isn't there, dividends following the shares actually held. Optionally holdings sell off pro-rata to cover overdrafts. Prices never change: your counterfactual selves don't move markets. Drag the marker on the net-worth chart to move the cutoff.
